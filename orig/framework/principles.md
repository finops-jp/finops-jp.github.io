# FinOps Principles

**FinOps Principles act as a north star, guiding the activities of our FinOps practice.**

These principles are in no particular order, and they are each important to FinOps success. We encourage members to understand and practice all of these principles.

![](https://www.finops.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/image-8.svg)

![](https://www.finops.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Collaborate.svg)

##### Teams need to collaborate

  * Finance, technology, product, and leaders work together to manage each technology category at the speed and granularity each requires.
  * Coordination from executives to engineers, and across all Personas ensures everyone is focused on the most valuable strategic goals. 
  * Teams work together to continuously improve for efficiency and innovation.

![](https://www.finops.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/BusinessValue.svg)

##### Business value drives technology decisions

  * Unit economic and value-based metrics demonstrate business impact better than just aggregate or categorized technology spend.
  * Make conscious trade-off decisions among cost, quality, and speed.
  * Use the strengths of each technology category to derive value and support innovation.
  * Differentiate with [FinOps Scopes](<https://www.finops.org/topic/scopes/>) to target the business value of specific organizational goals.

![](https://www.finops.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Ownership.svg)

##### Everyone takes ownership for their technology usage

  * Accountability of usage and cost is pushed to the edge, with engineers taking ownership of costs from architecture design to ongoing operations.
  * Individual feature and product teams are empowered to manage their own mix of technology usage against budgets they help control.
  * Decentralize the decision making around cost-effective architecture, resource usage, and optimization.
  * Technical teams must consider cost as a first class metric from the beginning of the software development lifecycle.

![](https://www.finops.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Reports.svg)

##### FinOps data should be accessible, timely, and accurate

  * Process and share cost data as soon as it becomes available.
  * Real-time visibility autonomously drives better technology utilization.
  * Fast feedback loops result in more efficient behavior.
  * Consistent visibility into technology spend is provided to all levels of the organization.
  * Create, monitor, and improve real-time financial forecasting and planning.
  * Trending and variance analysis helps explain cost trends.
  * Internal team benchmarking drives best practices and celebrates wins.
  * Use FOCUS and other methods to continuously improve normalization, accuracy and consistency in data across technology categories.

![](https://www.finops.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/CentralizedTeam.svg)

##### FinOps should be enabled centrally

  * A centralized FinOps function encourages, evangelizes, and enables best practices in a shared accountability model.
  * Executive level communication and business and technology strategies alignment is better enabled through a central team or function.
  * Rate, commitment, and discount optimization are best centralized to take advantage of economies of scale and allows Engineers to stay focused on optimizing their usage.

![](https://www.finops.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/VariableCost.svg)

##### Take advantage of the variable cost model of the cloud.

  * Each technology category — public cloud, data center, SaaS, Licenses, Data Cloud Platforms, etc. — entails cost models that have pros and cons for any business. 
  * An increasing amount of every technology category is becoming consumption-based, granular, and more variable. Use lessons learned in managing cloud in other categories.
  * The variable cost model of the cloud, the fixed cost model of the data center, or other models present opportunities to deliver more value.
  * Make technology decisions based not only on technical requirements, but also these cost model considerations.
  * Embrace just-in-time prediction, planning, and purchasing of capacity wherever valuable to reduce overall waste.
  * Agile iterative planning is preferred over static long-term planning in an increasing percentage of the technology estate.
  * Shift to more proactive system architecture and design with continuous adjustments over infrequent reactive cleanups.
